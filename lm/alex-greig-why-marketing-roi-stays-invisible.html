The ROI Clarity Method

I watched a client spend $847,000 on marketing last year. When I asked what drove their best month, they couldn't tell me. They knew their total spend. They knew their revenue went up. But they had no idea which $100 of that $847,000 actually worked.

Most businesses waste 60% of their marketing spend because they can't measure what actually drives sales. They're flying blind with expensive fuel.

The problem isn't the marketing. It's the measurement. Most companies track vanity metrics instead of money metrics. They celebrate website visits while their bank account stays flat.

Here's what I call the ROI Clarity Method. Four parts: Track, Tag, Test, Terminate.

Track means measuring actual sales, not clicks or impressions. Every lead needs a dollar value. Every campaign needs a profit number. No exceptions.

Tag means knowing exactly where each customer came from. Not just 'Google Ads' but which specific ad, keyword, and landing page. One Auckland plumbing company I worked with discovered that 80% of their profitable jobs came from just three keywords out of 200 they were bidding on.

Test means running controlled experiments, not just launching campaigns and hoping. Change one variable at a time. Measure the result. Keep what works, kill what doesn't.

Terminate means cutting losing campaigns fast and doubling down on winners. Most businesses keep bad campaigns running because they're emotionally attached to the creative or the strategy. Data doesn't lie. Feelings do.

A construction company in Wellington was spending $12,000 per month across five different marketing channels. We applied the ROI Clarity Method and discovered that two channels were generating all their profitable work. We killed the other three and doubled spend on the winners. Same budget, 180% more profit.

The hardest part isn't the method. It's admitting that half of what you're currently doing isn't working. But once you can see clearly what drives real results, scaling becomes simple math.
